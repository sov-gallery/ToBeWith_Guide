Rat

Thomas Van Linge

Thomas van Linge extends his long‑standing investigation of the uneasy border between the organic and the manufactured. The work consists of an ever‑growing cluster of hand‑crafted ceramic rats, each individually modeled, fired, and then coated in glossy nail‑polish. The lacquer, typically associated with personal adornment and fleeting fashion, transforms the bodies into hyper‑reflexive artefacts that oscillate between the clinical sheen of mass‑produced objects and the intimate, tactile presence of handcrafted sculpture.

The rats are posed in moments that betray a quiet finality, limbs splayed, heads tipped, tails curled, as if caught in the instant of death. Meanwhile the glossy finish imbues them with an unsettling serenity. By marrying the grotesque familiarity of a pest with the seductive allure of cosmetic polish, van Linge forces a confrontation with the paradox of beauty in decay.

Thomas van Linge is a multidisciplinary artist whose practice moves between sculpture, installation, sound and experimental publishing. His work interrogates the tension between natural materiality and the digital‐industrial economy, often presenting reclaimed industrial objects as “artificial landscapes” that mirror contemporary consumption patterns.
