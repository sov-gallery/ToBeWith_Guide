BEEST

Izzy Waller

BEEST reflects on relationships as a shared process of becoming. The work centers on intimacy between hybrid organisms, entities made of different materials, elements, and forms of life that coexist within a single structure. Through collaboration and communication, these hybrids explore what it means to collectively form a sense of self.

The work imagines relationships where transformation and growth happen together through ongoing entanglement. Identity is not fixed but shaped through interaction, care, and mutual adaptation. Fragility and resilience exist side by side as hybrid organisms learn to function as a whole without erasing its internal differences.

This work connects to Waller’s broader practice of “creaturization,” in which hybrid bodies create space for exploring new identities and ways of relating. It offers a quiet proposal for how we might coexist with others, with ourselves, and with the materials that shape us.

Izzy Waller's work centers on the human body. Fascinated by non-verbal communication and behavior, they investigate identity through the use of masks and bodily transformation. Their process is guided by intuition and experimentation, using diverse media to explore the boundaries between self and other, and to question social norms while imagining alternative futures.
