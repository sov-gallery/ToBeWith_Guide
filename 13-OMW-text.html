On My Way

Viktoria Vysotskaia

The viewer is confronted with a figure’s face sliding sideways into a dreamlike, surreal landscape. The perspective suggests that we may be looking at our own reflection from above — or at an oversized, deity-like figure from below. In either case, an eerie impression of an unclear power imbalance arises between the viewer and the figure facing them.

Whether it is a narcissistic sense of superiority or a deep insecurity in the presence of a demi-god that the viewer is asked to confront, the psychological unease is softened through Vysotskaia’s characteristic use of bright colors, distorted perspectives and proportions, glossy surfaces, and serene landscapes that conceal menacing promises of both glory and demise — a world existing in parallel to our seemingly quiet, predictable, and mundane everyday reality.

Towers of pearlescent spheres set against a lilac void render the image equally peaceful and terrifying. The work reminds us that we are always in transition, and that tranquility is merely the breath between states.

Vysotskaia is a visual artist and curator based in Berlin. In her artistic practice she delves into the hidden corners of our shared psyche, capturing moments where nostalgia, melancholy, and imagination collide. Her works serve as a portal to a world where the familiar blends with the surreal, urging viewers to confront their own memories, fears, and hopes.
