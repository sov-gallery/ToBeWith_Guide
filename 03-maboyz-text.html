Maboyz

Viktoria Vysotskaia

Conceptualized as an homage to Yushi Li's "My Tinder Boys" photography series, Vysotskaia plays with dating clichés and gender stereotypes within a plush, pink Y2K-aestheticized scene.

Three protagonists perform vulnerability, indifference, and independence, yet their poses betray a far more ambivalent relationship — both to the implied onlooker and to themselves. Insecurity, self-absorption, hesitation, and flight are aestheticized into a visual reminiscent of early 2000s teen magazines and boyband album covers — the primary sources of relationship advice that shaped Millennial culture.

The distinct, flashy aesthetic attempts to conceal the darker undertones of performative love that the fragile ego offers as a substitute for the real thing.

The work becomes a colorful, upbeat meditation on the inescapability of toxic pop culture presented to early-2000s teens, echoing the love songs and romantic films of that era.

Vysotskaia is a visual artist and curator based in Berlin. In her artistic practice she delves into the hidden corners of our shared psyche, capturing moments where nostalgia, melancholy, and imagination collide. Her works serve as a portal to a world where the familiar blends with the surreal, urging viewers to confront their own memories, fears, and hopes.
