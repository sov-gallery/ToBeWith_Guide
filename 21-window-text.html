Window

Polina Ryman

The garden was empty. Now it is abandoned. No one to look after it, no one to pick the cabbages and apples. No one to make chicken soup in the kitchen. No one to cut the lilies for Mama. The garden knew its owners would never return from the city. And the garden went wild. The once-living organism no longer moved in the wind. Everything was still. It became clear that movement was indeed life. The garden remained motionless—overgrown with weeds and darkened by the autumn sun.

The garden is a metaphor for purgatory. A place after life in which the hero finds themselves. Initially unaware of where they are and why, they wander through the garden of their childhood, finding objects from their past. Their grandmother, grandfather, and parents have left traces in the overgrown garden. When the hero discovers the traces in the garden, they realise that both they and their relatives are no longer alive.

Polina Ryman is a multimedia artist living and working in Berlin. Her practice explores the subtle fractures of everyday life, focusing on the unstable boundaries of time, language, and the uncanny. While drawing is central to her work—particularly graphite—she develops a transmedial approach, incorporating sound, embroidery, scent, and sculptural forms.
