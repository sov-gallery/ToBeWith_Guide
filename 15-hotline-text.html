Hotline

Claudia Redka

Telephony can bridge spatial and physical distances. It literally establishes a connection that allows you to feel close to the other one. At the same time, the disembodied voice also evokes longing when a friend or loved one is close and yet very far away.

As the first communication medium that made a real-time connection between sender and receiver possible, it is now a means of communication belonging to the pre-digital era. Landline telephones, telegraph poles, fax machines – all of these objects are increasingly shifting into the realm of technical artifacts.

The image of a landscape raked with cables is a childhood memory of car rides between Poland and Germany. Visible communication channels that connect people across borders. In this respect, the work also questions the relationship-building technology of telephony in its material and cultural contexts.

Where do connections to family exist, and where are they interrupted? How does closeness to a country manifest itself when one has never lived there, yet speaks its language?

Claudia Redka’s work deals with verbal and nonverbal communication processes in a cross-media manner. In the interaction of materials and techniques, she finds media-specific properties and the deformations, omissions, and gaps that arise during information transfer. The focus is on connections, tensions, and relationships between objects and people, the space in between, and the impossibility to say everything.
